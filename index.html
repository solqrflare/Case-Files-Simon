ENTRY 1 — CHAPTERS 1 & 2

FIRST IMPRESSIONS
BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATION

Simon faints during the first big meeting. Although physically weak, he sticks around, helping Ralph build shelters while everyone else runs off to play. He has no desire for recognition or reward, making little mention of his assistance.

TEXTUAL EVIDENCE — EXHIBIT 1-A
“He was a small, skinny boy, his chin pointed, and his eyes so bright they had deceived Ralph into thinking him delightfully gay and wicked.”
— Golding, p. 24
PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

Simon isn’t motivated by attention or power. Others desire leadership or enjoyment, however, Simon listens to his conscience, doing what he believes is correct. He persists, working all day, knowing he is the weakest in the group.

PSYCHOLOGICAL CONCEPT
Altruism

He helps others without the expectation of receiving something in return.

ANALYST PREDICTION — LIU, A.

He will stay silent, helping the group in the shadows. However, staying alone will make him an easy target later on.

ENTRY 3 — CHAPTERS 5 & 6

FEAR AND THE BEAST
BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATION

At the assembly about the beast, Simon tries to speak up and suggests that maybe the beast isn’t physical, but rather something inside them. The other boys laugh at him and he gets too embarrassed to finish his thought.

TEXTUAL EVIDENCE — EXHIBIT 3-A
“What I mean is… maybe it’s only us.”
— Golding, p. 89
PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

Simon understands what the other boys are unable to — that the real “beast” is the hidden nature of the boys themselves. He cannot explain it correctly to the others, as he stammers and trails off, and the group shuts him down before he can finish.

PSYCHOLOGICAL CONCEPT
Conformity

Everyone wants to believe in an actual monster they can hunt and kill, since it’s easier than admitting they’re the problem. Simon refuses to go along with the group’s answer.

ANALYST PREDICTION — LIU, A.

Simon will keep trying to look for the truth and help others understand it.

ENTRY 5 — CHAPTERS 9 & 10

MORAL COLLAPSE
BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATION

Simon discovers that the beast is actually a dead parachutist caught in the trees. He has a strange vision or hallucination involving the pig’s head (the Lord of the Flies), warning him of the “beast” inside him. He then stumbles down the mountain to tell the others the truth, but they’re in the middle of their ritual dance and kill him without realizing it’s him.

TEXTUAL EVIDENCE — EXHIBIT 5-A
“The beast struggled forward, broke the ring and fell over the steep edge of the rock to the sand by the water. At once the crowd surged after it, poured down the rock, leapt on to the beast, screamed, struck, bit, tore.”
— Golding, p. 153
PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

Simon attempts to save them by showing them the truth, but the boys are so caught up in their chant and dance that they are unable to see him as a person anymore. The other boys become similar to a mob or a pack of animals.

PSYCHOLOGICAL CONCEPT
Mob Mentality

When people get caught up in a group they stop thinking as individuals and only follow what others do.

ENTRY 6 — CHAPTERS 11 & 12

FINAL PSYCHOLOGICAL STATE
BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATION

Simon is gone, but he has a lasting impact throughout the rest of the book. Ralph thinks about him and feels guilty, while Jack’s tribe acts like the killing never happened or wasn’t their fault.

TEXTUAL EVIDENCE — EXHIBIT 6-A
“Ralph wept for the end of innocence, the darkness of man’s heart, and the fall through the air of the true, wise friend called Piggy.”
— Golding, p. 202
PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

With Simon gone, there is nobody left to keep the group in check. Ralph still remembers him and feels the weight of what happened, but he can’t do anything to change things. The other boys just suppress the memory because facing it would mean admitting what they did was wrong.

PSYCHOLOGICAL CONCEPT
Loss of Conscience

Simon was the group’s last morally intact member, and once he was gone, there was nothing left to stop the group’s descent into complete savagery.

FINAL PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE

SUBJECT: SIMON — COMPREHENSIVE ASSESSMENT
HOW DID THE SUBJECT CHANGE?

Simon doesn’t have a significant change in morals throughout the novel, which is the most important aspect about him. Every other character on the island becomes savage or struggles to hold themselves together as the situation falls apart. Simon is the only one who stays consistent from beginning to end.

He didn’t change much. He was quiet, observant, and morally strong at the start, and he stayed that way until he died. What did change was his understanding of himself and the others. By the middle of the book, he had figured out what the beast actually was, something the other boys never managed to do.

PSYCHOLOGICAL FORCES AT WORK

Simon was driven by his conscience rather than any need for approval or power. He had no desire for authority or rule. He was different from everyone else, but that is also why nobody really listened to him. He was quiet and weird, so that when he finally had something important to say, the group didn’t take him seriously. His introversion gave him insight that nobody else had, but it also kept him from being able to communicate that insight effectively.

WHAT DOES SIMON SUGGEST ABOUT HUMAN NATURE?

Simon tried to bring the boys the truth and they killed him for it. He wasn’t killed because he was “dangerous” or a threat — he was killed because the group had lost the ability to recognize good when they saw it, killing anyone that thought different from them.

Golding uses Simon to suggest that people can be good as individuals, but in a group dominated by fear and authority, they are unable to keep their own conscience intact.
